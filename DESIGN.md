# WORLDSEED Design

## One-line pitch

Slingshot the last living seed through the gravity wells of tiny dying planets, choose a route across each miniature system and awaken enough worlds to restore the Worldheart.

## Player promise

WORLDSEED should be understandable in five seconds, satisfying in fifteen, strategically interesting within two minutes and screenshot-worthy throughout.

## Player fantasy

The player carries the last spark of life through a constellation that has gone quiet. Every successful landing turns a dead planetoid into a living desk-toy diorama and creates a new place from which to continue the journey.

The seed is not conquering worlds. It is reconnecting them.

## Core shot loop

1. The seed rests on a restored world or tactical Seedstone.
2. Several reachable destinations are visible.
3. The player drags backwards to set direction and power.
4. The shared deterministic simulation draws the gravity-curved path and its predicted outcome.
5. Release launches the seed.
6. Landing awakens a dead world or activates a tactical small body; missing triggers fast recovery.
7. The landing location becomes the next launch position, changing the available routes.

## System loop

1. Enter a compact authored system containing roughly five to seven destinations.
2. Choose between safe, direct worlds and harder routes with stronger strategic or mastery value.
3. Restore enough worlds to charge the system's dormant Worldheart route.
4. Optionally awaken every world and collect the system's stardust challenge.
5. Complete the Worldheart jump to unlock the next authored system.

Restoration order matters because every awakened world becomes a persistent launch node. The system is therefore a navigational graph expressed entirely through physical space.

## Campaign shape

The campaign is a sequence of authored planetary systems rather than one unbounded procedural universe.

- **Prologue — First Light:** authored Meadow → Ember → Frost establishes the visual bar while Grove, Tide, a one-use Seedstone, the orbiting Wayfarer and a three-mote Arc prove branching routes, persistent launch nodes, deterministic timing windows and optional mastery.
- **System I — The Broken Belt:** introduces branching destinations, Seedstones and a predictable orbiting asteroid.
- **System II — The Wandering Garden:** develops moving launch windows, moons and optional route challenges.
- **System III — The Long Night:** combines longer gravity assists, tighter safe routes and stronger narrative stakes.
- **Finale — Worldheart:** asks the player to use the full route vocabulary before delivering the largest restoration in the game.

Names and world counts are provisional. Each system must earn its place through a distinct spatial problem and awakening identity.

## Meaningful decisions

Every shot should support at least one real decision:

- **Destination:** which world should be restored next?
- **Route:** take a safe direct arc, a gravity assist or a tactical Seedstone?
- **Timing:** launch now or wait for a deterministic moving obstacle to clear?
- **Mastery:** pursue optional stardust and close passes or prioritise safe progress?
- **Completion:** open the exit quickly or restore every world for a perfect system?

Do not add currencies or permanent upgrades merely to simulate depth. First prove that destination order, launch geometry and optional mastery are intrinsically interesting.

## Small bodies and asteroids

Asteroids should create readable choices rather than random punishment.

### Seedstones

- Small landable bodies that act as temporary launchpads.
- Provide shortcuts, setup angles or access to difficult worlds.
- Do not count as awakened worlds.
- Initially support one launch before crumbling or going dormant.
- Clearly communicate their remaining use.

### Orbiting hazards

- Follow deterministic authored paths.
- Appear in trajectory prediction at the time the seed would reach them.
- Mark predicted collisions clearly in red.
- Create timing windows and risky slingshots, not unavoidable surprise deaths.

### Stardust

- Optional pickups placed on expressive or risky curves.
- Never required for basic completion.
- Contribute to system mastery rather than an economy.
- Glow gold when the current shared-physics prediction will collect them, and state the promised pickup count before release.
- Remain collected through the current run, while Reset restores the complete challenge.

## Objectives and mastery

The basic objective is to restore enough life to open the route to the system's Worldheart. A system can award three simple emblems:

1. **Heart:** complete the system.
2. **Bloom:** awaken every full world.
3. **Arc:** complete the authored stardust or route challenge.

This supports replay without grinding, random drops or upgrade-tree maintenance.

## Shot feedback

Before release, the player should understand where the seed will go, what it will gain and what threatens it.

- Green endpoint: safe landing on an already restored launch node.
- Gold endpoint: landing will awaken a new world.
- Blue endpoint: tactical Seedstone or moon landing.
- Red marker: predicted collision or invalid landing.
- Small luminous markers: optional stardust or close-pass opportunities.

After flight, concise accolades such as **Clean Landing**, **Gravity Assist**, **Close Pass** and **Perfect Awakening** should celebrate skill without interrupting the next choice.

## Story

Story is delivered in fragments that coexist with play:

- one short line during or immediately after a world's first awakening;
- environmental landmarks that reveal meaning when restored;
- a brief system introduction and completion line;
- a growing constellation map that shows life returning.

Example awakening lines:

- Meadow: “The seed remembered rain.”
- Ember: “One spark had waited beneath the stone.”
- Frost: “Under the ice, the old ocean was still dreaming.”

The campaign premise is simple:

> The stars are going quiet. Carry the last living seed through the forgotten systems, gather their memories and return life to the Worldheart.

Avoid lore dumps, dialogue choices and conventional cutscenes.

## Signature restoration beat

Landing produces an impact pulse. Colour and life race around the sphere from the exact contact point, vegetation and landmarks grow in sequence, atmosphere blooms, motion begins, a story fragment appears and the music gains a layer. The wave must visibly wrap around the world's curvature.

Worldheart restorations should remix the same language at system scale rather than replace it with an unrelated spectacle.

## Presentation

- Stylised desk-toy dioramas with clean silhouettes and exaggerated curvature.
- Long-lens miniature-photography feel, restrained UI and strong rim lighting.
- The seed, destinations and prediction outcome remain readable at mobile scale.
- System framing communicates multiple choices without turning into a distant map screen.
- Sound communicates aim, release, close pass, impact, growth, failure, route opening and completion.

## Controls

The base game remains one-pointer/touch accessible:

- drag backwards from the seed;
- release to launch;
- use the same gesture on mouse, pen and touch.

Do not add a mid-flight action until branching routes and predictable obstacles have been tested without one. If later introduced, it must remain optional, legible and compatible with deterministic simulation.

## Success criteria

- A first-time player launches without explanation beyond the on-screen prompt.
- Prediction and live flight agree.
- At least two destinations are genuinely viable in the first branching system.
- A player can explain why they chose one route over another.
- Landing order visibly changes subsequent opportunities.
- Seedstones and asteroids add planning rather than confusion.
- Each restoration is distinct and improves the soundscape and story context.
- The campaign objective and current system objective are always understandable.
- The complete affected journey runs reliably on modern desktop and mobile browsers.
- The final Worldheart restoration clearly resolves the emotional and mechanical journey.

## Submission handoff

Submission readiness is part of finishing the game: keep its public build, credits, copy, screenshots, showcase video and release evidence current. The final external submission or publication remains a user-controlled action. An agent may prepare and explain the entry, but must receive explicit confirmation at the final Submit or Publish step before triggering it.
