# WORLDSEED Design

## One-line pitch

Drag and release a living seed through the gravity wells of tiny dying planets, landing on each one to bring its miniature world back to life.

## Player promise

WORLDSEED should be understandable in five seconds, satisfying in fifteen and screenshot-worthy in thirty.

## Core loop

1. The seed rests on a restored world.
2. The player drags backwards to set direction and power.
3. The shared deterministic simulation draws the predicted gravity-curved path.
4. Release launches the seed.
5. Landing awakens a dead world; missing triggers a fast recovery.
6. Restore every world to complete the journey.

## Design pillars

- **Tiny worlds are the mechanic.** Their scale, curvature and gravity create the play, not merely the backdrop.
- **One expressive input.** Pointer and touch use the same drag-and-release gesture.
- **Readable skill.** Prediction is trustworthy; good shots feel authored by the player.
- **Transformation is the reward.** Landing must produce a memorable, planet-wrapping restoration sequence.
- **Immediate retry.** Failure costs seconds, not momentum.

## World sequence

- **Meadow:** tutorial and vertical-slice benchmark; warm, inviting and instantly legible.
- **Ember:** stronger contrast, heat and geological motion; builds confidence with longer shots.
- **Frost:** cool atmosphere, crystalline silhouettes and the emotional final restoration.

The current three-world journey is the committed jam scope. Add content only after all three worlds meet the quality bar.

## Signature restoration beat

Landing produces an impact pulse. Colour and life race around the sphere, vegetation and props grow in sequence, atmosphere blooms, motion begins and the music gains a layer. The wave must visibly wrap around the planet's curvature.

## Presentation

- Stylised desk-toy dioramas with clean silhouettes and exaggerated curvature.
- Long-lens miniature photography feel, restrained UI and strong rim lighting.
- The seed remains bright and readable at mobile scale.
- Sound communicates aim, release, close pass, impact, growth, failure and completion.

## Success criteria

- A first-time player launches without explanation beyond the on-screen prompt.
- The trajectory preview and actual flight agree.
- Each restoration is visually distinct and improves the soundscape.
- The full journey runs reliably on modern desktop and mobile browsers.
- The final frame clearly communicates the Tiny Worlds theme.
